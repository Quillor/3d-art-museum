
### H01 — Prehistoric Cave Art

**Period / region:** c. 40,000–2,000 BCE; Europe, especially Ardèche, France.

**Palette / texture:** Charcoal black, smoke gray, warm ochre, hematite red, chalk white, and damp limestone beige. Use pitted limestone, calcite veils, soot-darkened pockets, clay floor, and mineral bloom. Pigments must look rubbed or sprayed into stone rather than painted onto a clean wall.

**Entrance:** A low, irregular limestone throat inspired primarily by Chauvet-Pont d’Arc, with a gentle reveal rather than a monumental portal. Reference Lascaux and Pech Merle through contour, ceiling compression, and animal-mark density, not copied murals.

**Flat hallway:** A meandering cave wall with shallow alcoves, stalactite edges, charcoal/ochre animal studies, hand stencils, low amber grazing light, and a dark earth floor. Include no modern masonry.

**Frames / floor / ceiling:** No conventional frames; use recessed rock niches, bone/wood-like mounting stakes only where appropriate for interpretive reproductions. Floor is compacted clay and fine grit. Ceiling is uneven limestone with concealed warm light tucked into crevices.

**3D assets:** Irregular limestone wall modules; ceiling shelves; shallow niches; calcite drips; clay floor patches; small scale markers; low protective barriers; discreet museum lighting housings.

**2D textures:** Limestone albedo/normal/roughness; soot pockets; ochre and hematite pigment stains; calcite streaks; clay/grit floor; hand-stencil and animal-mark hero decals with broken alpha.

**Accuracy note:** Present the art as archaeological interpretation, not as a claim that original cave surfaces can be reproduced exactly. Keep human access and conservation lighting visibly controlled.
