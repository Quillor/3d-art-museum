## Hero element
No. The room has no instantly-recognizable signature element — its most distinctive feature is the glowing "STEP INTO THE LIGHT" exit wall, which is a transition device, not a Middle-East-modern statement. The only readable signage is the far-end exit sign, fully legible in exit_in.jpg: "STEP INTO THE LIGHT" with the subtitle "The Middle East - return to the Grand Crossing". Distinctiveness vs americasmodern: this room currently reads as a generic white-cube, visually near-identical to the Americas modern room. Comparing memodern's wall_left.jpg/floor.jpg to americasmodern's wall_left.jpg/floor.jpg, the kits are the same part-for-part: identical cream plaster walls with a single black picture-rail line, identical white terrazzo with black-and-brass border strips, identical brass-and-black railings and black label plaques. The only Middle-East signals are the artworks themselves (Osman Hamdi Bey's The Tortoise Trainer on wall_left, the Golestan Palace Mirror Hall photograph on wall_right) plus the words "The Middle East" on the exit sign — there are no mashrabiya screens, no geometric tile, no Arabic typography, no brass lanterns, and no regional accent of any kind in the architecture or decor. Swap the paintings and the two rooms would be indistinguishable.
